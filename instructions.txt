===============================================================================
 HARDWARE SWAP GUIDE
 Replacing the simulator with a real AD8232 + Arduino Uno R3
===============================================================================

The app was built so this takes minutes, not a rewrite. The simulator and the
real board are two implementations of ONE interface (backend/ecg/source.py),
and both emit the same thing: raw 10-bit ADC counts at 1000 Hz. Filtering,
R-peak detection, the WebSocket protocol, the 3D heart, the audio and the CSV
export never learn which one is connected.

There is no code to edit. Follow the checklist.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 STEP 1 -- WIRE THE BOARD
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    AD8232 breakout          Arduino Uno R3
    ---------------          --------------
    GND               ---->  GND
    3.3V              ---->  3.3V        *** 3.3V, NOT 5V. 5V destroys it. ***
    OUTPUT            ---->  A0
    LO+               ---->  D10
    LO-               ---->  D11
    SDN               ---->  (leave unconnected)

Electrodes, Einthoven's Lead I:

    RED    (+)   left wrist,  or left chest below the collarbone
    YELLOW (-)   right wrist, or right chest below the collarbone
    GREEN  (REF) right hip or right ankle -- somewhere bony, away from muscle

Use FRESH gel electrodes. Dried pads are the single most common cause of a
noisy, wandering trace.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 STEP 2 -- UPLOAD THE SKETCH
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    1. Open  arduino/ecg_ad8232/ecg_ad8232.ino  in the Arduino IDE
    2. Tools > Board  ->  "Arduino Uno"
    3. Tools > Port   ->  your COM port (note which one)
    4. Upload
    5. CLOSE THE SERIAL MONITOR.

Step 5 is not optional. Windows gives a serial port to one process at a time.
If the IDE's Serial Monitor is open, Python cannot open the port and you will
get "Access is denied".

Sanity check before moving on: open the Serial Monitor at 115200 baud. You
should see a fast stream of numbers, roughly 480-540 when the electrodes are
attached and you are still. If you see "!" repeating, the AD8232 is reporting
leads-off -- re-seat the electrodes. Then close the monitor.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 STEP 3 -- CONNECT IN THE APP
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    1. Start the app as usual (see README.md, or run start.ps1)
    2. Click "Arduino" in the Signal Source panel
    3. The port list populates; the likely board is preselected and marked
    4. Click "Arduino" again to connect

That is the entire swap. The graph, the BPM readout, the beating heart and the
heart sounds all switch to live data with no restart. Click "Simulate" to go
back at any time.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 STEP 4 (ONLY IF NEEDED) -- CONFIGURATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every hardware setting lives in ONE file: backend/config.py. You should not
need to touch it, but here is what to change if your setup differs.

* Auto-detection picked the wrong port
      SERIAL_PORT = "COM5"          # was None
  Or just choose the right port from the dropdown in the UI -- an explicit
  choice always overrides auto-detection.

* You are outside North America (50 Hz mains, not 60)
      MAINS_HZ = 50.0
  This retunes the notch filter. Leaving it at 60 in a 50 Hz country leaves
  visible hum on the filtered trace.

* You wired 3.3V to AREF and uncommented USE_EXTERNAL_AREF in the sketch
      ADC_VREF = 3.3                # was 5.0
  Getting this wrong does not break anything, but the volts column in the CSV
  export will be scaled wrong.

* You changed the sample rate in the sketch
      SAMPLE_RATE = <your value>
  Must match SAMPLE_RATE_HZ in the .ino. If these disagree, the displayed BPM
  will be wrong by exactly their ratio.

* Your board is a clone with an unusual USB-serial chip that is not being
  detected
      SERIAL_AUTODETECT_HINTS = (..., "your chip name", ...)

Restart the Python server after editing config.py.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 STEP 5 -- IF THE SIGNAL LOOKS BAD
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The simulator is your reference. If the real trace looks nothing like it, the
problem is physical, not software.

  Flat line, value pinned near 0 or 1023
      The AD8232 is railed. Usually 5V on the 3.3V pin (check before it dies),
      or a floating reference electrode.

  Wild swings, no recognisable QRS
      Motion artifact. Sit still, rest your arms, keep the cable from swinging.

  Thick fuzzy band on the raw trace, clean filtered trace
      Mains hum. Normal, and exactly what the notch filter is for. Unplug the
      laptop charger to confirm it shrinks.

  Slow wandering baseline
      Breathing and electrode drift. The 0.5 Hz high-pass handles it; the raw
      trace is supposed to look like that.

  BPM reads roughly double the true rate
      The detector is counting T waves as R waves, which happens when the T
      wave is unusually tall relative to the QRS. Try swapping the RED and
      YELLOW electrodes to invert the lead.

  "Port opened but no data arrived in 3 s"
      Sketch not uploaded, wrong baud, or the Serial Monitor is still open.

  "Access is denied" / "could not open port"
      Another process owns the port. Close the Arduino IDE entirely.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 WHERE THE SWAP ACTUALLY HAPPENS (for the curious)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  backend/ecg/source.py         the ECGSource interface both sides implement
  backend/ecg/simulator.py      SimulatedSource  -- synthetic
  backend/ecg/serial_source.py  SerialSource     -- the real board
  backend/main.py               ECGPipeline.select() tears one down, starts
                                the other. ~20 lines. Everything downstream of
                                it is shared, and is already proven correct by
                                the simulator.

  Verify the shared path yourself at any time:
      cd backend
      .venv\Scripts\python.exe selftest.py

  That runs the real filter and the real R-peak detector against synthetic
  signal at ten heart rates and noise levels, and checks the recovered BPM
  against ground truth. Every check must pass before you go looking for a
  hardware fault.
